Technique study · Stage 2

Four rendering techniques, one subject (a chamomile sprig), three sizes: 128 px (card art), 64 px (shelf tile), 40 px (smallest icon). Everything is SVG generated in code. The stress test animates 32 copies of each and measures frame rate.

1 · Gilded Herbal

Engraved linework in gold leaf on ink-dark ground: hatching fills the petals, stippling models the centre.

Technique
<pattern> hatching + stipple clipped to shapes, a gold gradient stroke
Risk
Fine hatching fills in below 48px, so small icons need a simplified "seal" version

2 · Shadow Theatre

A cut-paper silhouette against a glowing backlit sky (after Lotte Reiniger). Detail lives in the cut-outs and the outline.

Technique
Solid silhouette + mask cut-outs, a radial backlight gradient
Risk
Similar outlines (chamomile vs mayweed) depend only on shape, so the differences must be exaggerated

3 · Cyanotype Herbarium

A pale photogram on Prussian blue (after Anna Atkins, 1843). Where the plant is thicker, the print is whiter.

Technique
Translucent stacked whites, a slight blur for the contact-print edge, a grain overlay
Risk
A monochrome world: the trait colours (especially blue Calm) need a white keyline to stand out

4 · Folk Woodcut / Riso

Bold carved shapes in two spot inks, slightly out of register, with printed grain and hand-made wobble.

Technique
Offset duplicate ink layers (multiply), grain via feTurbulence, outline wobble via feDisplacementMap
Risk
Displacement filters are costly when animated: apply them to static layers only

Stress test

Animates 32 copies of one technique for 2 seconds and reports frames per second. On a phone, under 50 fps means the technique must be used sparingly.
